Design Statement

Section: About & Philosophy

I'm Huda Hassoun, a UX researcher and designer living in Southeast Michigan. I am a 2026–2027 Fulbright award grantee and a graduate of the University of Michigan, where I earned my Bachelor's in Cognitive Science and Linguistics and my Master's in UX Research and Design. Professionally, I have experience working in the sectors of technology, education, international diplomacy, and public government. Outside my design work, I love reading, traveling, and language learning.

I believe one's lived experience and physical environment shapes their approach to design. Where I live, the culture I was raised in, the physical environment I occupied, and the communities I interact with and am a member of have all influenced what I prioritize in my designs. Design is where mechanics and functionality, or engineering, meet culture and philosophy, or ethnography, and intersect with beauty and aesthetics, or art and architecture.

As a designer living in Southeast Michigan, it is my duty to serve my Metro Detroit community while upholding equity and access for all. My approach to design is human-centered, sustainable, functional, and beautiful. People and their pain points are at the heart of my design process as active participants, and not only as end users. When designing solutions, it should always be done with care, purpose, and intention. I will not design for the sake of it, but only if I find a gap in functionality and believe I am the one to fill it.

Art and beauty are necessary for the human experience, and are why we stay alive; a design should be beautiful, distinguished, and invoke emotions. In my design philosophy, accessibility is not an afterthought of functionality in human-centered design, it is synonymous with it. If a designed solution is inaccessible, I do not believe that solution actually exists.

As a linguist, analyst, and now designer, I want to create designs that are both beautiful and inspired, without sacrificing accessibility or belonging.

As the old saying goes,
If a tree falls in a forest and no one is around to hear it, does it make a sound?
I raise you,
If a solution is designed and cannot be accessed, does it actually exist?

My lived experience, physical upbringing, academic journey, and professional career have allotted me power, privilege, and influence over design decisions. While these are not inherently negative things, they come with a responsibility in how they are exercised. I want to leverage these things in equating accessibility with functionality, community with the physical environment, and beauty as mandatory while designing sustainable solutions for human-centered problems.
