Case Study 02

A-Z of Arabic Coffee

A bilingual A–Z poster designed in Figma using a modular grid. Each letter of the English alphabet is paired with a corresponding aspect of the Arabic coffee making process.

Figma Graphic Design Modular Grid Typography Bilingual Design

01 · Concept

Design Concept & Message

A tribute to heritage, hospitality, and the ritual of Arabic coffee, this poster follows the journey of harvesting the beans to serving the first cup.

This poster was crafted to pay homage to my heritage. The red and green flower design on the finjān, or coffee cup, represents warmth, hospitality, and the value of making guests feel welcomed and at home. A finjān typically holds between 60–90 ml of coffee, encouraging guests to stay longer and drink more, but also to drink slowly as Arabic coffee is unsweetened and very high in caffeine.

The dellah, or coffee pot, is made of brass, steel, silver, or gold and is a representation of wealth but also hospitality. Gold represents countries along the Persian Gulf which pride themselves in hospitality. The crescent shape resembles a beak so coffee is poured and the sediments, grounds, and spices settle at the bottom. Its handle is also comfortable to hold because only one person will serve coffee during the entire evening.

The A–Z structure is easy for readers to follow, with each letter representing a different part of the coffee making process from harvesting the beans to serving your first cup. The poster alternates between the red and green of the finjān, and the parts of the process that are most uniquely Arabic are highlighted in the gold of the dellah.

Bilingual by design. Every card pairs an English term with its Arabic equivalent, using Noto Naskh Arabic, a typeface that supports Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, and Arabic alphabets, so neither language compromises the other.

04 · Typography

Typeface — Noto Naskh Arabic

Design Concept Poster

Designed by the Monotype Design Team (Mohamad Dakak, Nizar Qandah, and David Williams), Noto Naskh Arabic supports Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, and Arabic alphabets.

Noto Naskh Arabic is a Serif typeface developed in 2014 from Droid Arabic Naskh, which was released in 2010 as part of Google's Noto Project. "Naskh" (نسخ) means "copy" in Arabic and was popularized during the manuscript movement of the 7th century.

I chose it because Arabic script is an abjad writing system, or one that is consonant-based where vowels are left out. Arabic is written right to left and is a script, with the majority of letters connecting to the previous or the next one. Very few typefaces support the Arabic alphabet without compromising on style or linguistic history. This typeface represents the design concept clearly and effectively.

Audience: Anyone who uses Greek, Cyrillic, or Arabic alphabets; anyone who likes Serif typefaces; anyone using an abjad writing system; and anyone who wants to create clear, effective designs without sacrificing the linguistic integrity of the language they are producing content in.
